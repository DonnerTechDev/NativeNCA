Features

What NativeNCA does

You add your signature keys once. When a site asks for a signature, NativeNCA shows a window: who's asking, what to sign and with which key.

Works wherever NCALayer works

eGov, online banking and other sites that ask for NCALayer work with NativeNCA the same way. Nothing to set up.

Password only once

You type the key password when you add the key. After that you confirm with a fingerprint, your face or your Windows PIN.

See what you sign

Before signing you see the address of the site that asks and the document itself. If something looks wrong, just select Cancel.

Never mix up keys

Give each key a name and a photo: "Personal", "Sole proprietor", a company logo. Handy if you have several keys or work as an accountant.

Keys under lock

Keys and passwords stay on your PC only, encrypted. They aren't sent anywhere, not even to the developer.

Easy to install and update

No administrator rights needed. The app tells you when a new version is out, updates with one button, and you can go back to the previous version.

How signing works

What it looks like on a site

  1. You select Sign in with EDS or Sign

    On eGov, in online banking or any other service where NCALayer used to open.

  2. The NativeNCA window appears

    It shows which site asks for a signature and what exactly you're signing. You choose the key right there.

  3. Touch the sensor or enter your PIN

    Just like signing in to Windows. No key password needed.

  4. Done

    The site gets the signature, and the key itself stays on your PC.

Security

Your key stays with you

Your digital signature is your signature, and the app that keeps it must look after it. Here's how NativeNCA does that, in plain words. Technical details for experts are on GitHub.

Signing happens on your PC

The site only gets the finished signature. The key itself goes nowhere: not to the site, not to the internet, not to the developer.

Keys are encrypted

Keys and passwords are encrypted by Windows and open only under your Windows account. You can turn on an extra lock: then only your fingerprint, face or PIN opens them.

With this lock, even another program on this PC can't decrypt the keys without your confirmation

Nothing hidden

All the app's code is published. Any expert can check what it does and what it doesn't.

NCALayer: closed code
NativeNCA: open code

Secure link to the browser

For a site to reach the app safely, a special certificate is needed. NativeNCA creates its own on each PC, and it's no good for anything else.

NCALayer: the same certificate on every PC
NativeNCA: every PC has its own

Updates can't be forged

Every update is signed by the developer. The app checks the signature and won't install a file someone has swapped.

No tracking

The app collects no statistics and sends nothing to the developer. It goes online only to check for updates (you can turn that off) and to get a timestamp for a signature, if you turned timestamps on.

Good to know
  • NativeNCA is an independent app. It isn't made by НУЦ РК (the National Certification Authority) or JSC NIT and hasn't been certified by them.
  • Keep your key files and their passwords somewhere safe, as before. You'll need them if you move to a new PC or reset your Windows PIN.
  • Don't rush to uninstall NCALayer. If some site doesn't accept the signature, use NCALayer there and let us know.

Installation

Getting started

STEP 1

Download

Select Download below and extract the zip. Inside is a single file, NativeNCA.exe.

STEP 2

Install

Run NativeNCA.exe and select Install. No administrator rights needed. The app appears in the Start menu.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is this an official app?

No. NativeNCA is made by an independent developer, not by НУЦ РК or JSC National Information Technologies, and it hasn't been certified by them. Sites can't tell the difference: they talk to NativeNCA just as they talk to NCALayer, and the signature is made with your own key.

Does it work with eGov and banks?

Yes. Signing in with a digital signature and signing documents work the same as with NCALayer. If some site still doesn't accept the signature, let us know on GitHub and use NCALayer there for now.

Do I need to uninstall NCALayer?

No, keep it. But the two can't run at the same time, so NativeNCA offers to close NCALayer and stop it from starting with Windows. You can bring NCALayer back any time.

NCALayer doesn't work for me. Will NativeNCA help?

Most likely, if the problem is NCALayer itself: Java, its certificate, or it simply hangs. NativeNCA doesn't need Java and tells you itself what's stopping it from working.

But if an ad blocker (uBlock Origin, for example) stops the site from reaching the app, it gets in the way of NativeNCA too. Add eGov or your bank's site to the blocker's exceptions.

Which keys work?

The usual НУЦ РК signature keys stored as files, the ones you get from eGov or a public service center (files ending in .p12). Keys of individuals, sole proprietors and company employees all work.

Keys on USB tokens (Kaztoken, eToken, JaCarta) and on ID cards aren't supported yet.

Do I need Java?

No. Everything the app needs is inside its single file. There's nothing else to install.

Is it safe to keep keys in the app?

Keys and passwords stay on your PC only, encrypted, and open only under your Windows account. In settings you can turn on extra protection: then only your fingerprint, face or PIN opens them. Keys never go online.

The README describes the encryption in detail.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC". What should I do?

That's how Windows treats apps that few people have downloaded so far. Select More info, then Run anyway. Download NativeNCA only from this site or the project's GitHub page.

Why does the app install a certificate?

Without it the browser won't let a site reach the app. The certificate is created for your PC only and is good only for the link between the browser and NativeNCA. It can't be used for anything else.

Chrome, Edge and Firefox pick it up automatically. If Firefox was open during setup, restart it. You can remove the certificate in the app's settings.

How does the app update?

When a new version is out, NativeNCA lets you know. The update starts only when you select Update and takes a few seconds. Your keys and settings stay.

If you don't like the new version, settings have a Go back to the previous version button. You can turn off update checks there too.

What happens if I reset my Windows PIN?

Usually nothing. But if you turned on extra protection for keys with a fingerprint or PIN, the keys saved in the app can't be opened after a PIN reset. Add them again from the files. That's why you should keep your key files and passwords, as before.

How do I uninstall NativeNCA?

Like any app: Settings > Apps > Installed apps, or the Uninstall NativeNCA button in the app's own settings. The keys you added stay by default, in case you install it again. To delete them too, select the check box. The app never touches your original key files.

Is there a version for Mac or Linux?

No, NativeNCA works only on Windows 10 and 11.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. The app is free and has no ads. If you'd like, you can support development.

Support development

NativeNCA is free, with no ads and no tracking. Donations help add support for keys on USB tokens, test the app with new sites and buy a developer certificate, so Windows stops warning at startup.
